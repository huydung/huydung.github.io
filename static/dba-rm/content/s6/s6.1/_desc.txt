## Data Fundamentals

Data fundamentals establish the conceptual ground for all research inquiry by clarifying what constitutes raw information versus processed insights, and how data are categorized by origin and nature. Understanding these distinctions is essential for researchers to design collection strategies that yield valid, reliable evidence. This section covers the core definitions—data as unprocessed facts, information as derived meaning—and introduces the primary typologies researchers encounter: data sources (observational, experimental, simulation, derived) and ownership (primary data collected directly for a study versus secondary data drawn from existing sources).

By grounding data collection in these fundamentals, researchers can make informed choices about whether to conduct original surveys and interviews (primary data) or leverage existing organizational records and published studies (secondary data), and whether their collection approach will be observational (watching teams in action), experimental (testing interventions), or derived (synthesizing multiple sources). This foundation supports all downstream decisions about methodology, analysis, and interpretation.
