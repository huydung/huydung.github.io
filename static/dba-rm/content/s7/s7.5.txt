# Verifying Meaning

Verification strategies ensure that qualitative findings are credible, grounded in evidence, and not artifacts of researcher bias or misinterpretation. Rather than applying statistical hypothesis tests to confirm findings, qualitative researchers employ diverse strategies to build confidence in meaning: examining whether patterns are plausible, cohesive, and internally consistent; testing alternative explanations; comparing interpretations across cases and contexts; and triangulating evidence from multiple sources. Systematic verification through iterative coding cycles and member checking strengthens confidence that interpretations genuinely reflect participants' experiences rather than researcher assumptions.

## Integration: Noting Patterns, Plausibility, and Clustering

Integration strategies consolidate codes and themes by identifying overarching patterns and ensuring interpretations are plausible given the data. Noting patterns reveals frequencies and connections among codes; asking whether patterns are plausible checks logical coherence and alignment with theory; clustering groups related codes into coherent families. In qualitative analysis, integration means examining whether related codes cluster coherently into a theme—and whether this cluster is plausible given interview content and existing theory on the phenomenon.

## Differentiation: Contrasts and Partitioning Variables

Differentiation strategies highlight distinctions and variations that prevent overgeneralization. Examining contrasts reveals cases or segments that don't fit the dominant pattern, forcing refinement; partitioning variables by context shows whether patterns hold universally or are contingent. In multi-case organizational studies, differentiation means asking: Is the pattern the same across all cases, or does it differ by context, team size, cultural background, or leadership style? Finding that outcomes differ by organizational context represents valuable differentiation that complicates but enriches findings.

## Abstraction: Subsuming, Factoring, and Logical Chains

Abstraction strategies build conceptual coherence by grouping concrete findings into higher-order concepts. Subsuming particular observations under broader categories creates hierarchy and theoretical depth. Factoring identifies underlying common elements—what do disparate behaviors have in common? Logical chains test whether interpretations form coherent sequences without gaps. The progression from hundreds of initial codes to dozens of focused codes to 7-8 integrated themes exemplifies abstraction—progressively subsuming particulars into larger conceptual units while maintaining logical coherence.

## Testing: Representativeness, Triangulation, Member Checking, and If-Then Tests

Testing strategies validate findings by checking them against evidence, alternative perspectives, and logical implications. Representativeness examines whether patterns are found across many segments and cases or only in isolated instances, calibrating generalizability claims. Triangulation compares findings from multiple data sources or analysts, confirming that similar themes emerge independently. Member checking invites participants to review findings and confirm whether interpretations resonate with their experience. If-then tests pose logical questions: "If organizational safety drives performance, then high-safety teams should show higher performance than low-safety teams." Quantitative follow-up research partly serves this function: if qualitative themes are valid, then quantitative analysis should confirm significant relationships.

## Ruling Out Spurious Relations

Spurious relations appear causal but reflect confounding variables or selection bias rather than true causation. Testing for spuriousness involves asking: Could a third variable explain both variables of interest? In properly designed analysis, controlling for covariates addresses spuriousness, isolating focal relationships from rival explanations. Qualitative research strengthens causal inference by examining whether alternative explanations account for observed patterns.
