## Literature Review

A literature review is a systematic examination and synthesis of existing research, scholarship, and knowledge relevant to your research question. It is far more than a summary of sources; it is a critical analysis that identifies patterns, gaps, contradictions, and contributions across multiple works. The literature review establishes the foundation for research by demonstrating what is known, what remains unknown, and how your work fills a gap. For example, a literature review might reveal a fundamental contradiction: mainstream organizational theory assumes that hierarchical structures inhibit team innovation, yet context-specific studies in certain industries or cultures suggest a different dynamic. This contradiction becomes the impetus for empirical investigation. A strong literature review is not exhaustive but rather strategic, selecting credible, relevant sources that build a coherent argument about the state of knowledge and the necessity of your research. It operates across multiple types (narrative, systematic, meta-analysis) and requires critical evaluation at every step.
