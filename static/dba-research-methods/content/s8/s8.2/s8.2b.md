## Qualitative Reliability: Consistency of Process and Analysis

### Dependability: Is the research process systematic and transparent?

Dependability is qualitative reliability: Does the research process operate consistently, and could another researcher follow the same procedures and reach similar conclusions? Unlike quantitative reliability (which focuses on measurement instruments), dependability focuses on process reliability. Dependability is established by documenting all procedural decisions: interview protocols specify questions asked to all participants; coding procedures define how data segments would be classified; multiple coding cycles ensure that codes are reviewed, refined, and verified systematically rather than ad hoc.

### Case Study Protocol: Standardizing data collection and management

A detailed case study protocol documents data collection procedures, schedules, rules, and quality checks. A complete protocol includes: (1) recruitment criteria and consent procedures, (2) interview/observation guide with specific questions and follow-up probes, (3) transcription standards (e.g., verbatim, including pauses and tone markers), (4) data storage and confidentiality safeguards, and (5) coding definitions and procedures. Any researcher following this protocol would collect and analyze data consistently, enhancing dependability and allowing replication or auditing.

### Research Database: Centralizing and organizing all research materials

A research database organizes all raw data (transcripts, field notes, documents), analysis artifacts (codes, memos, theme definitions), and outputs (findings, conclusions). A well-organized database enables the audit trail—tracing any final conclusion back to its original data source—and allows independent verification of how findings were derived from evidence.

### Audit Trail: Documenting analytical decisions and reasoning

An audit trail records the reasoning behind key analytical choices: Why recode a segment differently in a later cycle? Why group certain codes into one theme? Maintaining reflexive memos documenting these decisions allows auditors to understand the logic and consistency of analytical choices. The audit trail demonstrates that theme identification followed documented reasoning from data rather than random or biased selection.

### Key Principle: Reliability = Consistency of Process

Across all these mechanisms, qualitative reliability ensures that research processes are systematic, transparent, and replicable. A dependable study allows readers to understand exactly how data were collected, analyzed, and interpreted, and gives confidence that another researcher following the same protocol would reach similar conclusions from similar data.
