## Qualitative Validity: Ensuring Trustworthiness and Authenticity

### Credibility: Does the study accurately represent participants' experiences?

Credibility is the qualitative equivalent of internal validity—it asks whether findings truthfully reflect the reality participants described. Credibility is established through prolonged engagement with participants across multiple interviews, allowing deep understanding of experiences. Two-cycle coding processes that refine initial codes into themes, combined with member checking—returning analyzed themes to participants for verification—confirm that themes genuinely capture lived experience rather than researcher assumptions.

### Transferability: Can findings apply to other settings or populations?

Transferability parallels external validity: Can insights from one context inform understanding in others? Rather than claiming findings apply universally, qualitative researchers provide thick description—detailed contextual details about organizational culture, norms, team composition, and setting. This allows readers to assess whether findings transfer to their own contexts, enabling judgments about applicability.

### Confirmability: Are findings grounded in data, not researcher bias?

Confirmability (qualitative objectivity) ensures findings are traceable to raw data and not merely researcher interpretation. Maintaining a chain of evidence—documenting how original data → coded segments → themes → conclusions flow logically and are auditable. Audit trails record decision points (why code an utterance this way? why assign it to this theme?), allowing independent verification that themes emerged from data rather than cherry-picking quotes to fit a predetermined narrative.

### Qualitative Validity Assurance Mechanisms

**Triangulation**: Using multiple data sources, methods, or analysts to converge on truth. Triangulating interview data with observational field notes and documentary evidence strengthens confidence that findings represent genuine patterns rather than just interview artifacts or researcher bias.

**Member Checking**: Returning themes and interpretations to participants for feedback. After initial analysis, themes are presented to participants, asking "Does this capture your experience?" Participant confirmation that findings accurately reflect their reality validates the analysis against researcher projection.

**Chain of Evidence**: Documenting the audit trail from raw data through analysis to conclusions. Maintaining detailed memos linking each coded segment to original data sources, theme definitions, and final conclusions makes the derivation of findings transparent and replicable by independent auditors.

**Case Study Protocol**: Establishing consistent procedures for data collection and analysis. Using a standard interview guide across all participants, consistent transcription protocols, and predetermined coding scheme definitions reduces drift in how data were interpreted and analyzed.
