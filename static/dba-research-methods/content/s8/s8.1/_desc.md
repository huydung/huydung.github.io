## Validity: Assuring Your Measures Reflect Reality

Validity is about accuracy: Does your measurement tool actually capture the construct you intend to study? In quantitative research, validity concerns measurement accuracy and the logical connection between variables. In qualitative research, validity (termed "trustworthiness") concerns whether findings authentically represent participants' lived experiences. Across both paradigms, validity has multiple dimensions—internal (causality within the study), external (generalizability beyond the study), and construct (accurate operationalization of concepts). In research, validity manifests differently across methods: in quantitative studies, convergent and discriminant validity confirm that scales measure distinct constructs reliably; in qualitative interviews, credibility safeguards like member checking ensure that themes accurately reflect participants' experiences.
