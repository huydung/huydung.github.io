## Collection Methods

Collection methods are the systematic procedures through which researchers gather data from respondents, participants, or organizational systems. The choice of method shapes what data are accessible, how complete and accurate those data will be, and what analytical approaches become possible downstream. Researchers select methods based on their research questions, available resources, time constraints, and the nature of phenomena under study. This section distinguishes quantitative collection methods—surveys, structured interviews, systematic observations, and secondary data reviews—from qualitative sources of evidence including documentation, archival records, semi-structured interviews, direct observation, participant observation, and physical artifacts.

Strategic method selection—whether employing surveys to reach many respondents efficiently, interviews to allow probing and clarification, observation to document actual behavior, or documents to provide historical context—ensures researchers gather evidence aligned with their scholarly aims. Understanding the full menu of available methods and their respective strengths and limitations enables researchers to design studies that yield credible, rich evidence.
