## Data Collection

Data collection is the systematic process of gathering information from various sources to answer research questions and support decision-making. In business research, effective data collection balances methodological rigor with practical constraints, drawing from multiple sources—surveys, interviews, observations, and organizational records—to build a comprehensive understanding of phenomena. The choice of collection method depends on the research design (quantitative, qualitative, or mixed), the research question, available resources, and the need for generalizability versus depth of understanding.

Data collection across diverse methods—surveys capturing numerical responses from many people, interviews revealing rich narratives from fewer participants, observations documenting behaviors in real time, and documents providing historical context—collectively enables understanding phenomena from multiple angles. This section explores the foundations of data collection—from distinguishing raw data from processed information, through quantitative and qualitative typologies, collection methods, survey design principles, and interview techniques—providing a roadmap for researchers to select and execute collection strategies aligned with their scholarly goals.
