## Problem & Question

A research inquiry begins with identifying a gap or problem worth investigating. This section guides you through articulating what problem you are investigating, why it matters, and what specific questions will drive your research. The problem must be grounded in existing literature while addressing a meaningful gap in knowledge. Examples include observing that certain workplace patterns contradict existing theory, discovering under-researched populations, or noticing inconsistencies between predictions and reality. The inquiry process moves from broad exploration to narrow, testable research questions, paired with hypotheses (quantitative) or propositions (qualitative) that predict or explain relationships. Together, these elements form the foundation for rigorous investigation.
