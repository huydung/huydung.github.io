## Presenting Findings: Communicating Results With Rigor and Impact

Presenting findings requires different approaches for quantitative and qualitative research, reflecting how each paradigm develops and validates knowledge. Quantitative reporting emphasizes statistical precision—effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values—and follows conventional structures (methods, results, discussion) that readers expect. Qualitative reporting emphasizes authenticity and richness—bringing readers into lived experience through narrative, detail, and quotation—while maintaining transparency about analytical processes. Both aim to communicate findings credibly: readers should trust that results are accurately reported, grounded in data, and thoroughly analyzed. Audience matters: presenting quantitative findings to non-researchers requires different language than presenting to fellow researchers; presenting qualitative findings to practitioners requires attention to actionable implications, while academic audiences need full methodological transparency.
