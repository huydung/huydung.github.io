# Mixed Methods Analysis and Integration

Mixed-methods research intentionally combines quantitative and qualitative data, analyzing each separately then strategically integrating findings to answer complex research questions that neither method alone could adequately address. Integration happens at multiple stages: research design (determining which method comes first), data analysis (analyzing quant and qual separately, then seeking connections), and interpretation (explaining convergence, divergence, and complementarity). Three primary sequential designs guide integration: explanatory sequential (quantitative first, then qualitative to explain mechanisms), exploratory sequential (qualitative first, then quantitative to test scale and generalizability), and convergent (both simultaneous, then merged). Mixed-methods research progressively deepens understanding by combining empirical breadth with narrative depth.

## Explanatory Sequential: Quantitative First, Qualitative Explains

Begin with a quantitative survey or experiment that identifies statistical relationships and effect sizes, then conduct qualitative interviews or observations to understand the mechanisms, contexts, and lived experiences that explain those relationships. The quantitative phase generates research questions for the qualitative phase: "Why do high-performing teams report higher safety than low-performing teams? Through what mechanisms?" Qualitative data then illuminates processes, conditions, and interpretations that explain the quantitative findings. Quantitative evidence identifies what happens; qualitative evidence explains how and why it happens.

## Exploratory Sequential: Qualitative First, Quantitative Tests and Extends

Begin with qualitative interviews, focus groups, or observations to explore a phenomenon, generate themes and hypotheses, then design a quantitative study to test whether those themes and hypothesized relationships hold at scale. This design is powerful for emerging research questions where literature is sparse and theory is underdeveloped. Qualitative exploration generates specificity; quantitative testing provides population generalizability. Preliminary qualitative observations identify promising mechanisms, which quantitative analysis then measures systematically across broader samples, testing whether observed patterns generalize.

## Convergent: Qualitative and Quantitative Simultaneous, Then Merged

Conduct quantitative and qualitative data collection and analysis in parallel, then deliberately merge findings to examine convergence (do both data sources support the same conclusions?), divergence (where and why do they differ?), and complementarity (what does each add?). Convergent designs are efficient and powerful for complex phenomena requiring multiple perspectives. In organizational research, quantitative surveys might measure psychological safety and engagement across teams while qualitative interviews capture team dynamics and leader behaviors. Merging findings reveals: Do high-performing teams report both high quantitative safety scores and qualitative experiences of safety? Where do quantitative and qualitative data align, and where do they diverge—and what do those divergences reveal?

## Joint Display of Qualitative and Quantitative Data

A joint display physically arranges quantitative and qualitative findings in proximity—typically in a table or diagram—to facilitate comparison and integration. Columns might display quantitative metrics (e.g., mean, correlation coefficient, effect size) alongside qualitative themes or quotations on the same rows, revealing relationships and convergences. A joint display table for organizational safety might show:

| Quantitative Finding | Qualitative Theme | Integration |
|---|---|---|
| Organizational culture strongly predicts team engagement (β=0.45, p<.001) | Culture shapes expectations and norms | Convergent: Both data sources confirm culture influences engagement |
| Engagement predicts performance (β=0.52, p<.001) | Engaged teams contribute discretionary effort | Convergent: Quantitative paths match qualitative mechanisms |
| Indirect effect is significant (VAF=28%) | Additional factors beyond culture influence engagement | Complementary: Quant identifies partial mediation; qual reveals contextual factors |

Joint displays make integration visible and verifiable, helping readers see how quantitative relationships align with or diverge from qualitative understanding.
