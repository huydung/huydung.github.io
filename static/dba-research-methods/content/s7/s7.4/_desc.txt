# Data Display

Once data are analyzed—whether through statistical tests, coding cycles, or integration of multiple methods—findings must be communicated through displays that make patterns visible, relationships transparent, and complexity manageable. Data displays range from simple quantitative tables and graphs to elaborate qualitative matrices and network diagrams to sophisticated joint displays that weave quantitative and qualitative evidence together. The choice of display depends on the analysis type and the audience: stakeholders might prefer a bar chart showing an intervention's effect on outcomes, while a research team might need a causal network diagram showing feedback loops in organizational dynamics. Effective displays make findings immediate, memorable, and actionable.

Both quantitative and qualitative displays follow a principle: reduce cognitive load by organizing complexity into visual or tabular forms that humans can quickly parse. A table of 146 survey responses is less useful than a summary table with mean scores, standard deviations, and correlations; a list of 300 coded interview segments is less useful than a thematic map showing how codes cluster into themes and relate to organizational outcomes.
