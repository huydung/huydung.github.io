# Quantitative Data Display

Quantitative displays translate numerical analysis into visual or tabular forms optimized for clarity and comprehension. Charts, graphs, tables, and numerical summaries organize patterns in ways that raw data cannot; a scatterplot immediately reveals correlation strength, while a clustered bar chart compares group means across multiple categories. In mixed-methods research, joint displays integrate quantitative and qualitative findings, revealing convergence, explaining divergence, and deepening interpretation.

## Charts and Graphs

Visualizations like bar charts, line graphs, scatterplots, and histograms communicate numerical patterns through spatial arrangement, reducing cognitive load. A bar chart showing mean performance across three organizational safety levels is immediately interpretable; a scatterplot of engagement versus performance reveals the strength and direction of association. In organizational research, a line graph tracking team cohesion across six months of team development shows trajectory; a histogram of engagement distribution across 146 respondents reveals skewness or bimodality. Well-designed charts guide the eye to key findings.

## Tables and Numerical Summaries

Structured tables organize statistics—means, standard deviations, frequencies, correlations—enabling precise reference and detailed comparison. A correlation matrix table displays relationships among all variables simultaneously; a descriptive statistics table summarizes each variable's central tendency and variability. In quantitative research, summary tables report means and standard deviations for all key variables; a correlation matrix shows bivariate relationships; a path coefficient table displays direct, indirect, and total effects with confidence intervals and p-values.

## Convergent Joint Display (Qual + Quant, Simultaneous Integration)

Merges quantitative and qualitative findings side-by-side, examining convergence or divergence. A convergent joint display might present correlation coefficients alongside thematic findings about the same relationship. In mixed-methods research, a convergent display might show the quantitative coefficient (organizational safety → performance, β=0.537) alongside qualitative themes (e.g., "Safe teams contribute ideas that improve outcomes," "Safety enables higher engagement"), demonstrating alignment and building a more complete picture.

## Explanatory Sequential Joint Display (Quant First, Qual Explains)

Presents quantitative findings first, then qualitative findings that explain or elaborate mechanisms underlying the statistical relationships. A contingency table shows which groups differ significantly on a measure; qualitative interviews then illuminate why those differences exist. In organizational research, quantitative analysis might reveal that employee engagement varies significantly across team types; qualitative interviews then explain the specific practices of high-engagement versus low-engagement teams, illustrating the pathways through which context influences outcomes.

## Exploratory Sequential Joint Display (Qual First, Quant Tests)

Presents qualitative findings first (themes, patterns, mechanisms), then quantitative tests that validate or extend those findings. Qualitative themes generate hypotheses that quantitative analysis tests at scale. Preliminary qualitative observations might identify promising mechanisms (e.g., leadership modeling improves team trust), which quantitative analysis then measures systematically across participants, confirming the hypothesized mechanisms in a broader sample.
