# Qualities of Research

Rigorous research shares several defining characteristics that distinguish it from opinion, anecdote, or guesswork. These qualities ensure that findings are trustworthy, can be evaluated by others, and contribute meaningfully to knowledge.

## Objectivity

Objectivity means findings are based on evidence, not personal preference or bias. A researcher studying psychological safety collects interview data from multiple team members using the same questions, rather than only interviewing people they like or those likely to agree with their hypothesis. Research measuring psychological safety uses validated scales applied consistently across all participants, ensuring results reflect actual patterns, not the researcher's beliefs.

## Neutrality

Neutrality requires presenting findings honestly, including unexpected or contradictory results. A researcher doesn't cherry-pick data that supports their initial expectations. For example, if research predicts that hierarchical structures inhibit team safety but finds the opposite in certain cultural contexts, publishing this counterintuitive finding rather than dismissing it as an anomaly exemplifies true neutrality.

## Empirical

Empirical research is grounded in observable, verifiable data—not speculation. Whether collecting survey responses, interview transcripts, or performance metrics, the evidence must be concrete and accessible to scrutiny. Qualitative studies rely on verbatim interview data or documented observations rather than relying solely on the researcher's interpretations.

## Replicable Steps

Replicable steps mean another researcher could follow your methodology and reach similar findings. You document your sampling method, data collection tools, analysis procedures, and decision rules so someone else could repeat or audit your work. Well-designed studies explicitly describe participant recruitment, questionnaire instruments, and analytical techniques—allowing other researchers to verify the approach or test it in different organizations.

## Comprehensive Analysis

Comprehensive analysis examines data thoroughly, considering multiple perspectives and alternative explanations. Rather than stopping at the first pattern you notice, you explore contradictions, examine edge cases, and test whether findings hold across subgroups. Strong qualitative research identifies multiple themes and perspectives, not just one dominant narrative, capturing the complexity of how different people experience phenomena across different contexts.

## Neutral Language

Neutral language avoids emotional, judgmental, or promotional tone. Instead of saying "The brilliant leader created a magical team culture," rigorous writing states: "Leaders who demonstrated credibility through technical knowledge and consistency were perceived as increasing team members' willingness to take interpersonal risk." Professional, evidence-based language maintains objectivity even when findings reveal important insights.
