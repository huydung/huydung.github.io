# Research Philosophy

Behind every research design lies a set of philosophical assumptions—beliefs about what is real, how we can know things, and what counts as evidence. These philosophical frameworks, or paradigms, shape everything from your research questions to how you interpret data. A researcher working within one paradigm might dismiss questions another paradigm finds essential.

Understanding research philosophy helps you recognize why different studies use different methods and why their conclusions may not contradict—they're answering different kinds of questions through different lenses. Different philosophical approaches—such as positivism (seeking statistical relationships), interpretivism (understanding meaning and lived experience), and pragmatism (testing what works in practice)—each illuminate different aspects of complex phenomena. None is "right"; each is appropriate to its research aim.
