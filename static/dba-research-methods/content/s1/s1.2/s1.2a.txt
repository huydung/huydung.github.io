# Classification Pairs

Research can be organized along several key dimensions, each highlighting a different purpose or approach. These classification pairs are not absolute categories—most studies blend elements of both—but they clarify research intent and design choices.

## Descriptive vs. Analytical

Descriptive research documents what exists: patterns, characteristics, frequencies. Analytical research explains why things happen by examining cause-effect relationships. Qualitative research is often largely descriptive—it maps themes and characteristics of how people experience phenomena. Quantitative research is often analytical—it tests whether one variable predicts another and measures the strength of that relationship using statistics. Often you need both: you describe a phenomenon first, then analyze its causes.

## Fundamental vs. Applied

Fundamental research pursues knowledge for its own sake, testing theories and building understanding. Applied research solves immediate, practical problems. Much organizational research leans heavily toward applied: investigating challenges directly relevant to business needs such as improving team effectiveness or developing training programs. Applied research can also contribute theoretically—findings may challenge or extend existing theory—but the research originates in organizational need rather than pure scholarly interest.

## Conceptual vs. Empirical

Conceptual research develops frameworks, refines definitions, and synthesizes existing knowledge through literature review and theory-building. Empirical research collects new data from the world—through surveys, interviews, experiments, or observation. Most rigorous research programs are predominantly empirical: they collect fresh data from participants or settings, then ground conceptual contributions in that empirical evidence. This makes theoretical insights credible rather than merely speculative.
