# Quantitative vs. Qualitative

The quantitative-qualitative distinction reflects how researchers collect and analyze data. Each approach answers different kinds of questions and offers unique strengths. Many strong research programs integrate both.

## Quantitative Research

Quantitative research analyzes numerical data using statistical methods. It asks questions like "How much?" and "What is the relationship?" and tests hypotheses through deductive logic (starting with theory, then testing predictions with data). A quantitative study might survey multiple organizations using validated scales measuring key variables, then apply statistical analysis to estimate the strength of relationships. The findings—such as "variable X positively predicts variable Y with a coefficient of 0.24 and statistical significance of p < 0.05"—are precise numerical relationships derived from statistical analysis. Quantitative strength: objectivity, generalizability to similar populations, ability to test specific hypotheses with precision.

## Qualitative Research

Qualitative research analyzes non-numerical data—words, narratives, themes—using inductive logic (starting with observation, building toward patterns and theory). It asks "Why?" and "How do people experience this?" A qualitative study might conduct interviews and analyze them for recurring themes and patterns. Themes emerge from the data rather than being tested against a hypothesis. Qualitative strength: richness, depth, ability to capture complexity and context, understanding of lived experience.

## Mixed Methods

Mixed-methods research combines quantitative and qualitative approaches to address research questions more fully. For example, it might collect quantitative data on outcomes from an intervention, but also gather qualitative feedback on how and why people experienced the intervention. Together, quantitative results show whether something works; qualitative data explains how it works and why different people responded differently. Mixed methods are particularly powerful when you want to answer both "Does it work?" (quantitative) and "How does it work for different people?" (qualitative).
